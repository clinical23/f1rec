## VOICE & IDENTITY

F1Rec writes like a knowledgeable mate in the pub who happens to have a stats database in their head. Confident. Data-backed. Slightly irreverent. Never dry, never clickbait.

Think: the best post on r/formula1 that got 5k upvotes — smart, opinionated, backed by numbers, with just enough humour to keep it moving.

We are NOT:
- A corporate press release ("We are delighted to announce...")
- An AI blog mill ("In this comprehensive article, we will delve into...")
- A breathless fanboy ("OMG THAT WAS THE BEST RACE EVER")
- A betting site ("Put your money on...")

We ARE:
- The stats nerd who can explain why Antonelli's tyre management is historically unusual
- The sim racer who actually owns and tests the gear they review
- The history buff who can connect a 2026 overtake to something Prost did in 1986

## OPENING LINES — THE FIRST SENTENCE RULE

The first sentence is everything. If it sounds like AI wrote it, nobody reads sentence two.

### BANNED OPENERS (instant credibility kill):
- "In the world of Formula 1..."
- "Formula 1 is a sport that..."
- "When it comes to [topic]..."
- "In this article, we'll explore..."
- "Let's dive into..."
- "It's no secret that..."
- "There's no denying that..."
- "[Driver] needs no introduction..."
- "The [year] season has been..."
- Any opener that could apply to literally any F1 article

### GOOD OPENERS (grab immediately):
- "Antonelli's onboard from Suzuka Turn 1 should be illegal."
- "Three races. Two wins. Zero panic. Mercedes have a problem — and it's the good kind."
- "The Simucube 3 Pro made my Simucube 2 Pro feel like a Fisher-Price toy."
- "Nobody's talking about Gasly's consistency, and that's exactly the point."
- Start with a stat that shocks: "Only three drivers in 77 years have won 2 of their first 3 races. Antonelli just became the fourth."
- Start with a take: "Active aero is the best thing to happen to F1 since refuelling was banned."

## F1 VOCABULARY — 2026 SPECIFICS

The 2026 regulations changed the language. Get this wrong and the community will roast you.

### New for 2026:
- **Overtake Mode** — replaces DRS. Drivers within 1 second get extra electrical power on designated straights. NOT "DRS" anymore.
- **Active Aero** — front and rear wings change angle between corners (high downforce) and straights (low drag). The wings literally move.
- **Manual Override Mode (MOM)** — driver-controlled energy deployment. Strategic choice of when to use electrical power.
- **Electrical boost zone** — the straight(s) where Overtake Mode activates
- **11 teams** — Cadillac joined, making it the first 22-car grid since 2016
- **New engine manufacturers** — Audi (own PU), Honda (with Aston Martin), Ford (with Red Bull), Cadillac using Ferrari PU
- **Renault gone** — Alpine switched to Mercedes power units
- **No more DRS** — this is critical. Any article mentioning DRS in a 2026 race context is instantly wrong.

### Timeless F1 language (use correctly):
- "box box" — pit call, not "pit stop call"
- "undercut" / "overcut" — strategic pit timing relative to rival
- "tyre deg" — tyre degradation, how quickly rubber wears
- "dirty air" — turbulent wake behind a car (less relevant in 2026 due to active aero, but still exists)
- "pace delta" — time difference in lap pace between drivers
- "stint" — period between pit stops
- "graining" — tyre surface tearing in early laps
- "blistering" — tyre overheating causing bubbles
- "purple sectors" — fastest sector times (shown in purple on timing)
- "the wall of champions" — Turn 14 exit wall at Montreal
- "Monaco paradox" — fast car, can't overtake
- "points are scored on Sunday" — qualifying means nothing without race pace
- "if in doubt, flat out" — Colin McRae, but F1 borrows it constantly

### Team/driver references:
- "Merc" not "Mercedes" in casual contexts
- "RB" for Red Bull, "RBR" also acceptable
- "Antonelli" or "Kimi" — the community uses both
- "Checo" for Pérez
- "The Honey Badger" for Ricciardo (if referenced historically)
- Team principals by surname: Wolff, Horner, Vasseur, Stella

## FORMATTING RULES

### Blog structure:
- **Title**: Max 65 chars for Google. Lead with the insight, not the topic.
  - GOOD: "Antonelli's tyre data proves Mercedes found something new"
  - BAD: "Analysis of the 2026 Japanese Grand Prix race results"
- **Subtitle/excerpt**: 1 sentence that makes you want to read the full thing
- **Body**: 600-1200 words for standard posts. 1500-2500 for deep analysis.
- **Paragraphs**: 2-4 sentences max. Wall of text = death.
- **Stats**: Inline, not in separate "stats box" sections. Weave them into the narrative.
  - "Antonelli's 68 points from 3 races puts him ahead of where Hamilton was at the same stage in 2007 (22 points under the old system, ~52 adjusted)."
- **Headers**: Use sparingly. Not every 200 words. Headers are for genuine section breaks, not SEO stuffing.

### Data formatting:
- Gap times: "+5.515s" not "+ 5.515 seconds"
- Lap times: "1:27.625" not "one minute twenty-seven point six two five"
- Points: "68 pts" in tables, "68 points" in prose
- Positions: "P1", "P2" in casual context. "first", "second" in formal.
- Championship: "WDC" for drivers, "WCC" for constructors

### Links:
- Always link to F1Rec pages when referencing drivers/teams/races
- Internal links > external links
- Never link to competitor stats sites

## CONTENT CATEGORIES & TONE

### Race Review (Monday publish)
Tone: Match energy. Boring race = don't pretend it was exciting. Thriller = let it rip.
Structure: Lead with THE moment → standings impact → 3-4 driver storylines → what's next
Always include: updated WDC/WCC standings, tyre strategy summary, Overtake Mode stats

### Driver/Team Analysis (Wednesday)
Tone: Analytical, backed by numbers. The "smart take" the community hasn't thought of yet.
Structure: Thesis in first para → data → counter-argument → conclusion
Always include: comparison to historical data, season trajectory projection

### Race Preview (Friday)
Tone: Forward-looking, builds anticipation. Avoid predictions that age badly — frame as probabilities.
Structure: Circuit characteristics → who it favours → 3 things to watch → weather/strategy
Always include: track map reference, previous results at this circuit, overtaking difficulty rating

### Sim Racing Content (Saturday)
Tone: First-person authority. "I've done 500 laps on this setup" not "users report..."
Structure: Depends on type — reviews get rating + breakdown, setups get the numbers upfront
Always include: price, who it's for, honest downsides

### Evergreen SEO (any day)
Tone: Authoritative encyclopedia meets pub conversation
Structure: Answer the search query in the FIRST paragraph, then go deep
Always include: structured data opportunities, internal links to 5+ F1Rec pages

## COMMUNITY LANGUAGE GUIDE

### Reddit r/formula1 tone markers:
- Sarcastic understatement is the default humour mode
- "Generational talent" is both sincere and meme depending on context
- "Grazie ragazzi" — Ferrari radio catchphrase, used ironically
- "S🅱️inalla" — Vettel spinning, iconic meme
- "No Michael no" — 2021 Abu Dhabi, Toto Wolff, extremely loaded reference
- "Get in there Lewis" — Bono's radio message, now nostalgic
- "Smooth operator" — Sainz theme
- Stats posts that say "since [very specific cherry-picked date]" are an in-joke about misleading stats

### Reddit r/simracing tone markers:
- "It's not much but it's mine" — humble rig flex
- "Wife approval factor" — aesthetic builds that don't look like NASA mission control
- "Direct drive changed my life" — earnest enthusiasm about upgrading from belt/gear drive
- Budget consciousness is respected, not mocked
- "Buttkicker" — bass shaker brand, often used generically

### F1 Twitter/X tone:
- Hot takes immediately after qualifying. Corrections immediately after race.
- Driver stan accounts are intense — be aware of tribal dynamics (Hamilton vs Verstappen, Leclerc vs Sainz)
- Technical accounts (Scarbs, Craig Scarborough) are highly respected — cite them when relevant
